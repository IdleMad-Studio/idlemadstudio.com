Hearwood

Select any text on your PC and hear it, right there.

Hearwood is a small Windows app that reads text aloud. Select text in any app and press Ctrl+Alt+S: natural voices read it on your own PC, with no cloud service and nothing sent anywhere.

  • Works offline, with the voices on your PC
  • No accounts, ads or analytics
  • Get it from the Microsoft Store
  • Source code on GitHub

What it does

Read from any app
Select text in a browser, a document or an email and press Ctrl+Alt+S. Hearwood reads it without opening a window, and puts your clipboard back as it was.
Change speed as it reads
From 0.75× to 3×, with the same pitch, from the window or with Ctrl+Alt+Up and Down.
Word highlighting
Hearwood's window shows what is being read and highlights each word as you hear it.
Fifteen voices
American and British, female and male, each named after a tree: Willow, Rowan, Hazel, Linden and more.
Your own hotkeys
Change the keys for reading, stopping and speed with Change Hotkeys, in the window or the tray menu.
Out of your way
Hearwood lives in the notification area. Its menu speaks the clipboard, pauses, stops, and switches voice or speed.
Start with Windows
Optional: Hearwood can start in the notification area when you sign in, ready for the hotkey.
Light and dark
Follows your Windows color setting, and stays sharp at any display scale.

Getting started

  1. Install Hearwood from the Microsoft Store and open it. Its icon appears in the notification area.
  2. Select text in any app and press Ctrl+Alt+S. Or type or paste text into Hearwood's window and press Speak.
  3. Choose a voice and speed at the top of the window. To use other keys, click Change Hotkeys.

Private by design

Speech is made on your PC by a model that comes with the app, so Hearwood works without an internet connection, and the text it reads is never sent anywhere or saved. Its settings stay on your PC. Read the full privacy policy.

Requirements

Windows 10 (version 1809 or later) or Windows 11, on an x64 or Arm64 PC, with about 375 MB of free space. On x64 PCs the processor needs AVX2, which most made since 2015 have; without it, Hearwood says that it can't run.
